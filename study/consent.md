## Purpose
This study compares two versions of a smartphone interface, one of which enlarges buttons automatically while you walk. We want to know which version is easier to use while standing and while walking.

## What you will do
- Answer a few background questions: age range, handedness, phone use.
- Do short tapping tasks on a phone (choose an app, find a contact, change a setting) in four blocks of about 4 minutes. Two blocks are done standing and two while walking at a normal pace along an indoor corridor.
- Rate how demanding each block felt, and answer a few questions about your preferences.

The session takes about **30–35 minutes**.

## Risks and discomforts
Using a phone while walking carries a small risk of bumping into something or tripping. To keep this risk low:
- the route is indoors, level, and free of stairs and obstacles;
- the researcher walks behind you;
- you may slow down, stop, or look up at any time.

You may feel mild fatigue.

## Benefits
There is no direct benefit to you. The results may help design phone interfaces that are easier to use on the move.

## Data and confidentiality
- The app records your taps (position and timing), task times, the phone's motion-sensor summary, and your questionnaire answers.
- It does **not** record your name, location, camera, microphone, or anything outside the study app.
- Data is labelled with a participant code (e.g., P07), not your name. This consent form is stored separately from the data.
- Only the research team for this course project will see individual data. Results are reported only in aggregate and may appear in a class report or presentation.
- Data will be deleted by [date].

## Voluntary participation
Participation is voluntary. You may skip any question and stop at any time without penalty. If you stop, you can ask for your data to be deleted.
